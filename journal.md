# MSc Business Analytics Journal

A collection of things I wish I knew before and during my MSc in Business Analytics.

The goal isn't to write every detail of my day—just one lesson, insight, mistake, or tip that could help someone else.

---

## Day 1 | 24 July 2026

### Topic
You don't need to know everything before starting your Master's.

### What I Learned
When I began my MSc, I thought everyone else would already be experts in Python, SQL, statistics, and machine learning.

The reality was different. Everyone had strengths in different areas and gaps in others. Some classmates were confident with coding but struggled with statistics, while others had strong analytical thinking but limited programming experience. The key lesson for me was to focus on steady progress rather than perfection. I found that consistent practice, asking questions, and building small projects helped me improve rapidly. Instead of trying to master everything at once, I prioritized the fundamentals that would help me in the next assignment or project and treated more advanced topics as things to learn over time. This mindset reduced anxiety and made learning more enjoyable and sustainable.

### Advice
Start before you feel ready. Progress beats perfection.

## Day 2 | 25 July 2026

### Topic
Your Master's is much more than lectures.

### What I Learned
I initially thought getting good grades would be enough. But over time, I realized that networking events, workshops, certifications, career fairs, volunteering, and maintaining a visible professional profile on LinkedIn all played a huge role in shaping the opportunities that came my way. Lectures teach concepts, but the extracurricular activities taught me how to apply those concepts in real-world contexts, communicate my ideas, and build connections with industry professionals and peers. Attending a single workshop or taking part in a student society sometimes led to internships, mentorships, or even simple referrals that opened doors later. Balancing coursework with these activities required discipline, but the investment paid off in both skills and relationships.

### Advice
Attend opportunities even when they seem optional. Sometimes the biggest career opportunities come from experiences outside the classroom.

## Day 3 | 26 July 2026

### Topic
Say "yes" to opportunities before you think you're ready.

### What I Learned
One of the first things I did after joining the University of Galway was express my interest in becoming a Global Student Ambassador. At that point, I didn't know exactly where it would lead. I simply knew I wanted to get involved beyond academics and make the most of my Master's journey. That decision introduced me to people from different backgrounds and gave me chances to develop public speaking, event coordination, and cross-cultural communication skills. Serving as an ambassador also helped me learn to represent my university professionally and gave me confidence in networking situations. Looking back, accepting that role early helped me grow faster than I would have if I had waited until I felt fully ready.

Sometimes the opportunities that shape your journey aren't part of your course; they are the extracurricular experiences you choose to pursue. Taking a role like this doesn't require perfection—just willingness, curiosity, and a readiness to learn on the job.

### Advice
Don't wait until your second semester or until you feel "settled." If something genuinely interests you, apply for it. The earlier you step out of your comfort zone, the more rewarding your Master's journey can become.

## Day 4 | 27 July 2026

### Topic
Prepare for your part-time job before you actually need one.

### What I Learned
One of the biggest lessons from my journey was that getting a part-time job starts long before your first interview. As soon as I landed in Ireland, I focused on completing all the essential paperwork—getting my Stamp 2 permission, IRP card, PPS Number, Leap Card, Revenue registration, and anything else required to legally work and live comfortably. I also prepared an Irish-style CV that emphasised relevant experience, clear formatting, and concise personal statements. My job search wasn't limited to a single platform; I checked Indeed regularly, visited supermarket and retail websites, joined local WhatsApp and Facebook groups, followed community pages on Instagram, and walked into stores to ask if they were hiring. Preparing these documents and searching consistently made me ready the moment an opportunity came up.

The biggest takeaway was that readiness matters: luck helps, but preparation and persistence matter more. Being proactive about paperwork and applications reduced stress and allowed me to seize opportunities quickly when they appeared.

### Advice
Don't wait until your savings are running low before you begin.

✔️ Complete your essential documents first. ✔️ Prepare an Irish-style CV. ✔️ Apply through company career pages and job portals. ✔️ Keep an eye on social media and local student groups. ✔️ Don't hesitate to walk into stores and ask if they're hiring.

The more places you look, the more opportunities you'll find.

## Day 5 | 28 July 2026

### Topic
Sometimes your first job isn't your dream job, but it's the one that changes everything.

### What I Learned
When I arrived in Ireland, I had a clear picture of the kind of part-time job I wanted. Coming from a vegetarian background, I wasn't comfortable working around meat or food preparation and preferred roles in bookstores, retail, customer service, or similar. Over the first few weeks, I applied everywhere and attended multiple interviews—the first one I remember was at Tesco (Ballybane). Although I wasn't selected, each interview taught me something about how to present myself, answer questions, and follow up professionally.

At the same time, I continued taking on volunteer roles and university opportunities, which kept my confidence up. Eventually, toward the end of October 2025, I came across a hiring advertisement for Subway. It wasn't the job I had pictured for myself, but after weeks of searching I decided to accept an interview and then the role. The Salthill restaurant was a brand-new franchise; I trained at another branch in Newcastle and then moved to the new store. The job pushed me outside my comfort zone—handling food types I had avoided before and adapting to a fast-paced service environment.

Despite initial nervousness, the role taught me valuable transferable skills: clear communication with customers and colleagues, teamwork, time management during busy shifts, and resilience in problem-solving. Those skills translated well to academic group projects and later professional settings. Saying "yes" to this unexpected opportunity was one of the most important decisions of my journey because it helped me build confidence and practical abilities I still use today.

### Advice
Don't reject an opportunity just because it isn't your ideal job.

Your first job doesn't have to define your career—it simply has to help you take the first step. Every experience teaches you something, builds resilience, and opens doors to new opportunities.

## Day 6 | 29 July 2026

### Topic
Balancing academics, work, and university life isn't easy but it's possible.

### What I Learned
One of the biggest challenges during my MSc wasn't the coursework itself—it was learning how to balance everything at once. My course lasted only nine months, which meant assignments, presentations, group projects, and deadlines came one after another. At the same time, I was working part-time at Subway and learning every part of the job: memorising recipes, understanding food safety procedures, serving customers quickly, handling the till, and preparing orders during peak times.

Those first few weeks were overwhelming, and I sometimes felt exhausted by the end of the day. Coming from a vegetarian background, I had never handled meat before and that added an extra layer of discomfort that I had to work through with training and support from colleagues. Outside of work and academics, I continued my involvement as a Global Student Ambassador and volunteered as a LIFT Ireland Facilitator, which helped me build leadership and facilitation skills.

There were days when lectures were followed by work, assignments had to be completed late at night, and weekends disappeared into deadlines. Over time I learned to prioritise tasks, set realistic daily goals, and create a weekly schedule that reserved blocks of time for study, work, and rest. Small routines—like prepping meals, limiting late-night work, and using a shared calendar for group projects—made a huge difference. Managing these responsibilities taught me discipline, resilience, and time management skills that no classroom alone could have taught me.

### Advice
Don't expect life during your Master's to be perfectly balanced every day.

Some weeks will be focused on assignments. Some on work. Some on university events.

Learn to prioritise, stay organised, and remember to take care of yourself along the way. Progress is about consistency, not perfection.

## Day 7 | 30 July 2026

### Topic
Starting over doesn't mean leaving everyone behind.

### What I Learned
One thing I underestimated before moving to Ireland was how much I would miss the people around me. Back home, I had friends I had grown up with—people who knew me without any explanation. We had shared countless memories, celebrations, late-night conversations, and everyday moments that I had taken for granted. Moving to a new country meant leaving that comfort behind and starting from zero.

In the beginning it felt strange and sometimes lonely—everyone around me was new, and I was unfamiliar to them. Building friendships took time and often happened through classes, group assignments, university events, and casual conversations. Slowly, a few people became more than just classmates. They celebrated small wins with me, supported me during assignment deadlines, shared meals after lectures, and helped me explore the city. What I realised is that making friends as an adult requires effort and vulnerability; you have to be willing to start conversations and accept invitations even when it feels uncomfortable.

The friends I have back home remain irreplaceable, but I'm grateful that Ireland gave me the chance to build another small circle that made this journey memorable. Maintaining relationships across distance also taught me how to be a better friend—I learned to communicate intentionally, schedule calls, and share life updates so those bonds stayed alive.

### Advice
Stay connected with the people you leave behind, but don't close yourself off to the people you meet along the way.

Sometimes a simple "Hi," joining a group project, or accepting an invitation for coffee can be the beginning of a lifelong friendship. Use tools like video calls, messaging apps, and social media to keep in touch and nurture those relationships.

## Day 8 | 31 July 2026

### Topic
My first paycheck in Ireland wasn't just money—it was independence.

### What I Learned
The day I received my first salary from Subway, I looked at my bank account with a smile I can't really describe. It wasn't about earning a huge amount of money; it was about the feeling that I was slowly building a life for myself in a new country. That paycheck meant I could contribute towards rent and electricity bills without constantly worrying, buy groceries with my own earnings, and enjoy small treats like a coffee after a long day.

It also gave me the freedom to take part in cultural experiences that made Ireland feel more like home—visiting the Galway Christmas Market, celebrating Halloween with friends, and taking short trips to nearby cities. Of course, there were responsibilities too: every month I had to budget for rent, utilities, groceries, transport, and savings. Learning to prioritise expenses and set aside a small savings buffer taught me valuable financial discipline.

Looking back, my first paycheck wasn't just a salary; it was a turning point that showed me I was capable of taking care of myself far from home. That confidence made it easier to focus on studies and explore new opportunities.

### Advice
Your first paycheck isn't a reward to spend all at once—it's the beginning of learning how to manage your own life.

Enjoy it. Celebrate it. But also learn to budget, save a little, and spend on experiences that make your journey memorable.

The memories from a weekend trip, a festive evening with friends, or a meal shared after a long week often stay with you longer than an impulse purchase.

## Day 9 | 1 August 2026

### Topic
A Master's teaches you more than academics—it teaches you how to work with people.

### What I Learned
When I started my MSc in Business Analytics, I had one goal in mind—to graduate with a First Class Honours (1:1). I knew it wouldn't be easy, especially with a course that lasted only nine months.

The pace was intense. Assignments, projects, presentations, exams, and part-time work all had to fit into the same calendar.

One challenge I didn't expect was group assignments.

Within a very short time after joining the university, we had to form groups of four or five students for almost every module. Since we barely knew each other, most groups were formed randomly.

Although we were all from India, we came from different states, universities, educational backgrounds, and ways of thinking. Everyone had different priorities, working styles, and expectations. Our very first assignment had its fair share of disagreements and misunderstandings.

There were times when I felt not everyone was contributing equally. Instead of letting that affect the quality of the work, I chose to focus on what I could control. If I felt the team was giving 50%, I pushed myself to give 150%.

That didn't mean doing everything alone. It meant communicating, negotiating, dividing responsibilities, resolving conflicts, and making sure we submitted work that we were all proud of.

Those experiences taught me that teamwork isn't about finding perfect teammates—it's about learning how to work with different personalities while keeping the end goal in mind.

Looking back, earning my 1:1 wasn't just about studying hard. It was about staying consistent, taking responsibility, and learning how to collaborate under pressure.

### Advice
Not every group project will be smooth, and not every teammate will work the way you do.

Focus on communication, mutual understanding, and delivering your best. Your attitude towards teamwork will often matter just as much as your technical skills.
